## Reading (MacBook Pro M5 Pro, 15 cores = 5 "Super" + 10 "Performance", 48 GB, AC power)

- **`taskpolicy -b` (background QoS)** caps total throughput at ~5 GB/s (≈1.5 default cores),
  independent of thread count: 0.13–0.37× of default. Rejected as a default prefix.
- **`taskpolicy -c utility`** is also capped, and erratic (0.66× at 8 threads, 0.31× at 12). Rejected.
- **`nice -n 10`** keeps full throughput (0.99–1.00×). Its cost to interactivity is small:
  - at 12 threads: timer-jitter p99 2.0 ms vs 1.1 ms baseline; Python start +4 ms; `git status` +6 ms;
  - at 4 threads: no measurable cost.
- **Scaling** is near-linear to 12 threads (2.98 vs 3.05 GB/s per thread), so the 10 Performance
  cores are not a bottleneck at that width.

**Defaults chosen:** focus = 4 threads, away = 12 threads, both launched with `nice -n 10`.
Memory budgets are unchanged; this benchmark does not exercise memory.

**Caveats:**
- The load is compute-bound and cache-resident (SHA-256). Memory-bandwidth-heavy simulations may
  interfere more.
- The interactivity proxies are synthetic. Re-check against real VS Code and browser use before
  raising `focus`.
